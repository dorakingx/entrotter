# Market, pricing and distribution hypotheses

Entrotter's initial customer hypothesis is an EVM agent team with two to ten
engineers, already using local forks and bespoke scripts. The proposed user is the
engineer evaluating an agent decision; the potential buyer is the person responsible
for safe release and reproducible reviews. Team size, buying roles and pain severity
are hypotheses, not findings from customer interviews.

## The job to test

“When I change an agent's transaction policy, help a teammate reproduce the
observations and outcomes so we can review the change before granting production
permissions.” The product must make a real review task easier than preserving a
script and its inputs. A polished report alone is not evidence that it does.

The measured alternative is a small direct-Anvil script. Its execution was about
as fast as Entrotter in the same six-run task. The additional proposed value is
explicit scenario constraints, comparable artifacts, causal decision records and
replay that refuses changed observations. Coding time, onboarding effort and
review accuracy have not been compared; measure them with real developers.

## A falsifiable pricing experiment

Keep the MIT engine, local execution and local report viewer free. Explore paid
team scenario management and shared review workflows only if users need them.
The interview anchor is **$99 per team per month**, with a comparison question
about a one-time support purchase. This is not an available plan, an offer, a
revenue forecast, validated willingness to pay or authorization to build paid
hosting. No payment, subscription, account or commercial SaaS exists in this scope.

Ask about existing workflows and costs before exposing the number. Ask what a
team would stop using, who would approve spending and what concrete evidence they
would need. A positive hypothetical answer is not a purchase or commitment. Do
not turn three interviews into a market-size estimate or conversion percentage.

## Distribution experiment

Start with three individually approved developer evaluations. Supply the public
local example and a pinned source revision, then observe reproduction of a case
chosen by the participant. Record install prerequisites and setup time separately
from experiment execution. Publish useful failure scenarios and reproducibility
notes only with permission and after removing private data. Let those artifacts
support future community discovery; do not claim partnerships or adoption.

After three sessions, decide whether to improve local reproducibility, investigate
team review needs, change the initial audience, or stop the paid hypothesis. A
hosted version would require a separately approved security and infrastructure
plan. Outreach drafts are unsent; owner approval is required before contacting
anyone or posting to a community.

## Decision criteria

The next milestone is observed behavior: three genuine sessions; an uncoached
attempt to reproduce one local result per participant after prerequisites; at
least one participant-owned, shareable scenario; and an explicit record of why
each participant would keep or abandon the tool. These are learning targets, not
results. Record failures and setup friction in full. Do not replace missing user
feedback with agent-generated personas, synthetic interviews or internal tests.
